AREA OF WORK

Our project focuses primarily on the specialized interdisciplinary domains of CLINICAL NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING (NLP), RETRIEVAL-AUGMENTED GENERATION (RAG), and SAFEGUARDED CLINICAL DECISION SUPPORT SYSTEMS (CDSS), integrated within modern high-performance software engineering architectures.

While Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated extraordinary capabilities across general-domain textual synthesis, their application to clinical medicine—specifically diagnostic breast imaging and radiology—presents acute challenges regarding safety, factual hallucination, and legal liability. In clinical radiology, report drafting cannot tolerate ungrounded assertions, arbitrary category inferences, or linguistic discrepancies between findings and management recommendations. The American College of Radiology (ACR) Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) establishes a strictly formalized, lexicon-governed ontology designed to standardize lesion descriptors, assessment categories (BI-RADS 0 through 6), and management pathways.

A critical vulnerability of standard generative AI applied to breast imaging is the risk of subtle yet catastrophic hallucinations: generating plausible-sounding recommendations that directly contradict standard BI-RADS clinical management, inventing non-standard descriptive terminology, or failing to maintain strict label isolation between diagnostic ground-truth and generative decision paths. Furthermore, radiologists face an increasing administrative documentation burden, while patients often struggle to understand complex diagnostic reports written in dense medical terminology.

To resolve these critical challenges, our area of work investigates and delivers the RP5 Architecture: a CPU-optimized, four-stratum retrieval-augmented generation assistant. The system implements:

  • Four-Stratum Authoritative Knowledge Representation: Stratum 1 (BI-RADS Lexicon), Stratum 2 (ACR Guidance and Management), Stratum 3 (Standard Structured Report Templates), and Stratum 4 (De-identified Demonstrative Cases), maintained with an immutable cryptographic provenance tracking system.
  • Dense Vector Space Semantic Search: Section-aware chunking combined with Sentence-Transformers embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and FAISS/NumPy indexing to ensure low-latency, relevant evidence retrieval.
  • Dual-Head Specialized Generation: Simultaneous generation of strict Pydantic-validated JSON structured reports for clinicians and plain-language summaries calibrated to a Flesch–Kincaid reading grade ceiling (≤ 8.0) for patients.
  • Multi-Factor Attribution & Clinical Conflict Resolution: Claim-level decomposition with combined lexical overlap and embedding cosine similarity verification, coupled with explicit clinical rules to detect negation contradictions, laterality mismatches, and lesion size measurement discrepancies.
  • Clinical Guard Agent & Deliberate Abstention Gate: A deterministic safety gate that enforces descriptor allowlisting, recommendation harmony, minimum citation coverage, and composite confidence thresholds, ensuring that the system reliably chooses SAFE ABSTENTION over ungrounded hallucination.

1. ABSTRACT

Clinical breast imaging reporting requires strict adherence to standardized nomenclature and management protocols established by the American College of Radiology (ACR) Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS). While generative Large Language Models (LLMs) offer promising efficiency in automating radiological documentation, their unconstrained deployment risks severe diagnostic hallucinations, recommendation inconsistencies, and legal liability. Furthermore, standard zero-shot models frequently suffer from "label leakage" during evaluation and fail to provide intelligible, health-literate translations for patients.

This project presents RP5, a safeguarded, CPU-optimized, BI-RADS-grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) assistant designed for structured breast imaging report drafting and patient communication. The architecture is founded upon five core engineering innovations: (1) a four-stratum authoritative knowledge corpus separating lexicon terminology (S1), clinical practice parameters (S2), structured report templates (S3), and de-identified few-shot exemplars (S4), tracked through an immutable cryptographic provenance log; (2) section-aware chunking paired with Sentence-Transformers dense embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and FAISS indexing, enforcing strict evaluation label isolation; (3) dual-head generation synthesizing structured clinician drafts adhering to a validated Pydantic JSON schema alongside patient-centered lay summaries constrained to a Flesch–Kincaid reading ceiling (≤ Grade 8.0); (4) a multi-tier sentence-level attribution engine verifying claim support through token overlap and semantic similarity thresholds (≥ 0.45) while enforcing deterministic filters against negation, measurement, and laterality conflicts; and (5) a deterministic Clinical Guard Agent implementing a multi-stage validation gate that prioritizes deliberate safe abstention over hallucinated generation whenever confidence falls below threshold (τ = 0.50), descriptors violate allowlists, or categories contradict clinical guidance.

The system was empirically validated through comprehensive unit testing (25/25 passed test cases), primary clinical vignette evaluation, multi-temperature consistency benchmarking (15 runs across T ∈ {0.0, 0.3, 0.7}), and adversarial red-teaming across three perturbation families (out-of-corpus, conflicting categories, and distractor injections). On non-abstained cases, RP5 achieved a Gwet’s AC1 inter-rater agreement score of 1.00, a mean citation coverage of 100%, and an average patient readability of Grade 6.65. Under adversarial stress, the guard agent safely abstained on 77.78% of malicious probes, limiting the residual empirical hallucination rate to 16.67%. The entire pipeline executes with a steady-state amortized inference latency of 310.2 ms, demonstrating that verifiable grounding and principled abstention provide a robust foundation for trustworthy AI in medical imaging.

Keywords: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), BI-RADS, Breast Imaging, Clinical NLP, Hallucination Auditing, Safe Abstention, Health Literacy, Medical AI Safety.

2. INTRODUCTION

2.1 Background and Clinical Context

Breast cancer remains the most diagnosed malignancy and a leading cause of cancer mortality among women globally. Early detection through routine screening mammography has been conclusively shown to reduce breast cancer mortality by 20% to 40%. In contemporary radiology workflows, the interpretation and documentation of screening and diagnostic mammograms rely upon the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS), published and standardized by the American College of Radiology (ACR). BI-RADS establishes a universal lexicon for describing breast composition, lesion morphologies, calcification distributions, and associated features, culminating in a standardized final assessment category ranging from BI-RADS 0 (incomplete assessment requiring further imaging) to BI-RADS 6 (known biopsy-proven malignancy).

Each BI-RADS category carries mandatory, evidence-based management recommendations. For example, a benign finding (BI-RADS 2) requires routine screening intervals, a probably benign lesion (BI-RADS 3) dictates short-interval (6-month) follow-up, and suspicious abnormalities (BI-RADS 4 or 5) mandate tissue biopsy. The precision of this vocabulary is vital: ambiguous phrasing, unapproved descriptive terminology, or internal contradictions between the descriptive findings and the numerical assessment category can delay vital interventions or cause unwarranted invasive procedures and severe patient anxiety.

2.2 Motivation: Pitfalls of Generative AI in Radiology

The exponential rise in screening imaging volume has led to a global shortage of breast imaging radiologists, resulting in severe diagnostic burnout, extended reporting turnaround times, and heightened administrative fatigue. Simultaneously, the advent of generative Large Language Models (LLMs) has prompted widespread interest in automating clinical documentation. However, standard commercial and open-weights LLMs exhibit critical vulnerabilities when applied directly to clinical radiology:

  • Diagnostic Hallucination: Generative models routinely fabricate non-existent lesions, attribute findings to the incorrect anatomical breast quadrant or laterality, or invent non-standard descriptive terminology absent from the ACR lexicon.
  • Clinical Contradiction: LLMs frequently suffer from internal incoherence, such as describing a classic circumscribed benign cyst but recommending urgent core biopsy, or declaring a study "Negative" while assigning BI-RADS 4.
  • Evaluation Label Leakage: In naive AI architectures, the reference ground-truth diagnosis is often inadvertently leaked into the prompt context, artificially inflating perceived diagnostic accuracy during research benchmarks.
  • Health Literacy Gap: Traditional radiology reports are formulated in dense, technical medical jargon. When patients access these reports via electronic health record (EHR) portals, the lack of accessible, plain-language explanations induces severe psychological distress. Conversely, naive LLM paraphrasing often oversimplifies or misstates critical medical facts.

2.3 The RP5 Paradigm and Proposed Contributions

To resolve these fundamental challenges, this project formulates and implements RP5 (BI-RADS-Grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation Assistant). The core guiding philosophy of RP5 is that in safety-critical medical AI, unprincipled generation is unacceptable: the system must generate only what is strictly grounded in authoritative evidence, verify all claims through multi-tier attribution alignment, and deliberately choose SAFE ABSTENTION whenever clinical uncertainty or contradictory data is detected.

The primary research and engineering contributions of this project include:

  1. Four-Stratum Knowledge Architecture: Implementation of an isolated, four-stratum knowledge base (S1: Lexicon, S2: Guidance, S3: Templates, S4: Exemplars) with an immutable cryptographic provenance log (provenance.jsonl), ensuring zero contamination between patient examples and authoritative guidance.
  2. Strict Label Isolation Protocol: Complete decoupling of reference evaluation labels from prompt construction (reference_label_in_prompt: false), guaranteeing that diagnostic agreement measurements reflect genuine clinical reasoning rather than trivial memory retrieval.
  3. Dual Clinician/Patient Generation Heads: Simultaneous synthesis of structured JSON reports conforming to strict Pydantic schemas for radiologists, and plain-language patient explanations constrained to an automated readability ceiling of Flesch–Kincaid Grade ≤ 8.0 with iterative regeneration capability.
  4. Multi-Factor Attribution and Conflict Engine: A sentence-level claim alignment module that combines lexical overlap with dense embedding cosine similarity (≥ 0.45) while enforcing deterministic clinical rules against negation contradictions, laterality mismatches, and lesion measurement errors.
  5. Clinical Guard Agent with Deliberate Abstention: A multi-gate validation engine enforcing category-to-recommendation harmony, descriptor allowlisting, citation coverage thresholds (≥ 70%), and composite confidence floors (τ = 0.50). When violations occur, the system safely emits structured Abstention objects rather than ungrounded claims.
  6. Adversarial Red-Teaming Benchmark: Comprehensive auditing under three stress families (out-of-corpus terminology, conflicting clinical categories, and distractor context injections), providing empirical proof of safe abstention behavior.

3. LITERATURE REVIEW

3.1 Evolution of ACR BI-RADS Standards

Prior to the establishment of the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) by the American College of Radiology in 1993, mammography reports suffered from widespread stylistic variability, ambiguous terminology, and conflicting management advice. Radiologists frequently utilized idiosyncratic descriptive phrases such as "somewhat suspicious nodule" or "indeterminate density," leaving referring surgeons and primary care physicians uncertain regarding whether to perform biopsy or continue screening.

The contemporary 5th Edition of the ACR BI-RADS Atlas [1] provides a standardized dictionary of morphological descriptors and definitive assessment categories. Lesions must be categorized into distinct morphological types: masses (characterized by shape, margin, and density), calcifications (characterized by morphology and distribution), architectural distortion, and asymmetries. Each finding is synthesized into a final assessment category ranging from Category 0 through Category 6, as delineated in Table 1.

Table 1: ACR BI-RADS Assessment Categories and Clinical Action Protocols

BI-RADS 1 Negative Essentially 0% Continue routine annual screening mammography

Extensive clinical trials by Sickles et al. [2] and D'Orsi et al. [3] have validated that adherence to BI-RADS significantly improves positive predictive values and reduces inter-radiologist variability. Consequently, any automated AI system designed to draft mammography reports must operate strictly within the formal boundaries of this standardized lexicon.

3.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in Healthcare

Standard parametric Large Language Models, such as GPT-4 [4], Llama-3 [5], and Med-PaLM 2 [6], store factual knowledge implicitly within their trained neural network weights. While capable of fluent biomedical text generation, parametric models exhibit severe limitations in specialized clinical domains: their knowledge cannot be easily updated without expensive fine-tuning, they do not cite verifiable clinical evidence, and they frequently confabulate believable yet factually inaccurate assertions—a phenomenon widely known as hallucination [7].

To resolve these parametric limitations, Lewis et al. [8] introduced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). In a RAG architecture, an input query is utilized to retrieve top-$k$ relevant document passages from an external, non-parametric knowledge corpus using dense vector embeddings. The retrieved passages are subsequently injected into the prompt context of an autoregressive generator model. In clinical domains, dense retrieval is typically powered by bi-encoder transformer models such as Sentence-BERT [9] or specialized clinical encoders such as PubMedBERT [10] and BioClinicalBERT [11]. The bi-encoder computes semantic representations of queries and documents:

score(q, d) = cos(E(q), E(d)) = (E(q) · E(d)) / (||E(q)|| · ||E(d)||)

Dense vector indexing libraries such as FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) [12] enable millisecond-scale nearest-neighbor retrieval over tens of thousands of clinical document chunks. Recent work by Zakka et al. [13] on Almanac demonstrated that grounding clinical LLMs with medical reference guidelines significantly reduces ungrounded treatment recommendations. Similarly, Tu et al. [14] demonstrated that domain-specific retrieval improves clinical reasoning across diverse diagnostic benchmarks.

3.3 Hallucination Risks, Attribution, and Auditing in Clinical AI

Despite the incorporation of retrieved context, clinical RAG systems remain vulnerable to subtle forms of hallucination. As formalized by Min et al. in FActScore [15], generative models frequently produce non-parametric hallucinations when they extrapolate beyond retrieved evidence, fail to resolve contradictory passages, or suffer from distractor context injection [16]. In radiology report generation, Huang et al. [17] identified that standard text similarity metrics such as BLEU [18] and ROUGE-L [19] correlate poorly with diagnostic accuracy, as a single inverted word (e.g., "no mass" vs "mass") produces high n-gram overlap but diametrically opposite clinical decisions.

To address these semantic limitations, Jain et al. [20] proposed RadGraph, a clinical entity and relation extraction graph framework tailored for radiology reports. Concurrently, adversarial red-teaming methodologies, such as Med-HALT [21], have emphasized the necessity of auditing clinical models under perturbed inputs, including out-of-distribution terminology and conflicting medical assertions. However, most existing frameworks merely measure hallucination rates retrospectively without providing real-time, deterministic guardrails capable of enforcing safe abstention.

3.4 Patient-Centered Communication and Health Literacy

The 21st Century Cures Act mandated immediate electronic release of clinical notes and diagnostic radiology reports to patient portals across the United States. While promoting clinical transparency, this policy revealed a profound health literacy gap [22]. The average reading literacy of adult populations in North America and Europe is estimated between the 6th and 8th-grade level, whereas standard mammography reports routinely exceed a 14th to 16th-grade reading level (college graduate level) [23].

The standard quantitative metric for assessing text accessibility in healthcare communication is the Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL) formula [24]:

FKGL = 0.39 * (total_words / total_sentences) + 11.8 * (total_syllables / total_words) - 15.59

Recent studies by Sridhar et al. [25] demonstrated that naive LLM translation of radiology reports often introduces severe factual drift, accidentally downplaying cancer risks or omitting vital follow-up recommendations. Consequently, an optimal clinical assistant must maintain a dual communication architecture: producing rigorous, unabbreviated structured reports for radiologists, while simultaneously generating verified, health-literate summaries constrained to an FKGL ceiling (≤ Grade 8.0) for patients.

4. PROBLEM DEFINITION AND OBJECTIVES

4.1 Formal Problem Statement

Let $\mathcal{C}$ represent a standardized clinical finding card extracted from a mammographic examination, denoted as:

C = {case_id, modality, laterality, breast_density, L, pathology, reason}

where $\mathcal{L} = \{l_1, l_2, \dots, l_m\}$ represents a set of identified lesions. Each lesion $l_i$ is characterized by its morphological kind (e.g., mass, calcification, architectural distortion), anatomical location, measured size in millimeters ($s \in \mathbb{R}^+$), and a list of descriptors $\mathcal{D}_i = \{d_{i,1}, d_{i,2}, \dots\}$.

The clinical reporting task consists of generating a dual-output tuple $(\mathcal{R}_{clin}, \mathcal{R}_{pat})$:

  1. A structured clinician report $\mathcal{R}_{clin} = \{view, findings, final\_assessment, recommendation\}$, where $final\_assessment \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ corresponds to the ACR BI-RADS assessment category, and $recommendation$ represents the evidence-based management action.
  2. A patient-facing plain language narrative $\mathcal{R}_{pat}$ explaining the findings and management steps, subject to the readability constraint $\text{FKGL}(\mathcal{R}_{pat}) \le 8.0$.

However, conventional generative models operating on this formulation suffer from three acute failure modes:

  • Failure Mode 1 (Ungrounded Assertion): Generating descriptors $d \notin \mathcal{K}_{lex}$ or recommending actions $a$ unsupported by authoritative guidance $\mathcal{K}_{guide}$.
  • Failure Mode 2 (Category Contradiction): Synthesizing an assessment category $y \in \{0..6\}$ that directly contradicts the recommendation $a$ (e.g., $y=1$ with recommendation for urgent biopsy).
  • Failure Mode 3 (Label Leakage & False Accuracy): Exposing ground-truth diagnostic labels in the prompt context during automated benchmark evaluations, yielding falsely inflated agreement scores.

4.2 Research and Engineering Objectives

To resolve this problem, the primary objectives of the RP5 system are established as follows:

  1. Architectural Stratification: Construct an isolated four-stratum knowledge base (Lexicon $\mathcal{S}_1$, Guidance $\mathcal{S}_2$, Templates $\mathcal{S}_3$, Exemplars $\mathcal{S}_4$) with cryptographic provenance logging to prevent cross-stratum data contamination.
  2. Strict Label Isolation: Enforce complete isolation of ground-truth reference labels during prompt serialization to guarantee rigorous, uncompromised evaluation.
  3. Multi-Factor Attribution Engine: Implement an attribution aligner combining lexical overlap and dense embedding cosine similarity (≥ 0.45) with deterministic conflict detection rules (negation, measurement, and laterality).
  4. Principled Clinical Guard Agent: Implement a deterministic multi-gate validation gate enforcing schema validity, descriptor allowlisting, category-recommendation harmony, citation coverage (≥ 70%), and composite confidence (τ ≥ 0.50), prioritizing SAFE ABSTENTION over ungrounded generation.
  5. Dual-Head Health-Literate Generation: Enable simultaneous generation of Pydantic-validated clinician JSON reports and patient explanations strictly complying with an automated Flesch–Kincaid Grade ≤ 8.0 ceiling.
  6. Empirical Rigor: Validate the system through 100% test suite passing, primary clinical vignette evaluation, multi-temperature consistency benchmarking, and adversarial hallucination auditing.

5. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY AND WORK DESCRIPTION

5.1 End-to-End System Pipeline

The RP5 architecture implements a modular, six-stage pipeline designed for deterministic execution, low-latency CPU operation, and comprehensive medical safety. Figure 1 illustrates the end-to-end data flow and architectural interactions.

Figure 1: End-to-End RP5 System Architecture detailing input card ingestion, dense retrieval, dual-head generation, attribution alignment, and the clinical guard agent.

The workflow proceeds across six formalized operational stages:

  1. Stage 0 (Finding Card Ingestion & Normalization): The input finding card is parsed and sanitized. Measurements are normalized to millimeters (converting centimeters and cleaning textual artifacts), invalid density codes are removed, and the evaluation reference label is strictly isolated.
  2. Stage 1 (Dense Vector Retrieval): The normalized card is serialized into query $q$ and transformed into a 384-dimensional embedding via all-MiniLM-L6-v2. A FAISS inner-product vector index retrieves the top-$k=8$ evidence passages across Strata S1–S3, while metadata filtering extracts Stratum S4 few-shot exemplars.
  3. Stage 2 (Dual-Head Generation): The Clinician Head synthesizes a structured JSON draft report adhering to a strict Pydantic schema. Simultaneously, the Patient Head translates the draft into a patient-accessible explanation targeting an FKGL ≤ 8.0 readability ceiling.
  4. Stage 3 (Attribution Alignment): Draft sentences are decomposed into atomic claims and aligned against retrieved passages via token overlap, cosine similarity (≥ 0.45), and explicit negation/measurement/laterality conflict filters.
  5. Stage 4 (Clinical Guard Evaluation): A multi-gate validation engine evaluates schema validity, descriptor allowlisting, recommendation harmony, citation coverage (≥ 70%), and composite confidence (τ ≥ 0.50).
  6. Stage 5 (Gated Output Delivery): If all gates pass, the dual reports are emitted with full attribution provenance. If any gate fails, a structured Abstention object is returned.

A foundational design principle of RP5 is the strict stratification of clinical knowledge. Rather than concatenating disparate medical texts into a monolithic database, RP5 partitions knowledge into four distinct strata, as illustrated in Figure 2 and specified in Table 3.

5.3 Section-Aware Chunking and Dense Vector Indexing

Conventional RAG implementations employ naive fixed-token sliding-window chunking (e.g., 500 tokens with 50-token overlap). In structured medical texts, naive chunking routinely cleaves cohesive clinical definitions across chunk boundaries, separating lesion descriptors from their associated pathological significance.

RP5 resolves this limitation by introducing a Section-Aware Chunker (rp5/corpus/chunker.py). The chunker operates via header-directed boundary extraction: it scans documents for clinical section headers (e.g., MASS, CALCIFICATION, ASSESSMENT CATEGORIES, RECOMMENDATION), isolating each clinical category as an atomic chunk. Only when an individual section exceeds the maximum configured chunk size ($chunk\_size = 512$ tokens) is recursive sliding-window splitting applied with configured overlap ($chunk\_overlap = 64$ tokens).

For vector indexing, RP5 utilizes the sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 bi-encoder. This model maps textual chunks into a compact 384-dimensional dense vector space, achieving an optimal trade-off between semantic retrieval quality and sub-50ms CPU execution. Chunks are indexed using FAISS Inner Product (IndexFlatIP) over $L_2$-normalized vectors, which mathematically equates to cosine similarity:

sim(u, v) = (u / ||u||_2) · (v / ||v||_2) = cos(theta)

To maintain operational resilience across lightweight deployment environments where compiled C++ FAISS binaries may be unavailable, RP5 implements a transparent fallback to a pure NumPy vector engine (rp5/retrieval/index.py) without altering retrieval rankings or threshold dynamics.

5.4 Dual Generation Heads (Clinician & Patient)

RP5 decouples report synthesis into two specialized generation heads tailored for distinct clinical consumers:

5.4.1 Clinician Head (Structured JSON Report)

The Clinician Head (rp5/generation/heads.py) enforces strict schema compliance. The prompt instructs the LLM to output a raw JSON dictionary containing exactly four keys: view, findings, final_assessment, and recommendation. Output validation is performed using a robust iterative JSON extractor and Pydantic schema validator. To guarantee diagnostic integrity, reference labels are omitted from prompt context, forcing the model to infer BI-RADS categories solely from retrieved morphological evidence.

5.4.2 Patient Head (Health-Literate Communication)

The Patient Head translates the clinician's findings into compassionate, jargon-free English. The generation prompt explicitly targets an educational reading level of 6th to 8th grade. Upon generating a draft, the system dynamically calculates the Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level using textstat. If the computed score exceeds the safety ceiling ($FKGL > 8.0$), the system triggers an automated simplification regeneration cycle with explicit linguistic feedback. If simplification fails after configured retries ($readability\_max\_regens = 1$), the pipeline safely halts or flags the text for clinician review.

5.5 Attribution Engine & Clinical Conflict Policy

A primary technical contribution of RP5 is the Multi-Tier Attribution Engine (rp5/attribution/aligner.py). Unlike post-hoc heuristic citation tools, RP5 evaluates claim grounding through multi-factor mathematical and clinical verification, as illustrated in Figure 3.

Figure 3: Multi-Tier Attribution Alignment and Contradiction Resolution Flow.

The attribution engine processes draft statements through three sequential verification tiers:

  1. Claim Decomposition: The raw text is tokenized into individual sentences using regex-aware clinical sentence tokenizers, producing atomic claims $C = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\}$.
  2. Dual-Evidence Scoring: For each claim $c_i$ and retrieved passage $p_j$, two metrics are computed:
    • Lexical Jaccard Overlap: Ratio of shared clinical content words between $c_i$ and $p_j$.
    • MiniLM Cosine Similarity: Semantic vector similarity $\cos(E(c_i), E(p_j))$. A claim is considered prima facie supported if $\cos(E(c_i), E(p_j)) \ge 0.45$ and lexical overlap is non-trivial.
  3. Clinical Contradiction & Conflict Filters: Even if semantic similarity is high, the claim is rejected if it violates any of three clinical domain rules:
    • Negation Inversion: If claim $c_i$ asserts the absence of a finding (e.g., "no mass observed") while evidence passage $p_j$ describes its presence, the attribution is flagged as negation_contradiction.
    • Measurement Mismatch: If claim $c_i$ cites a lesion size differing from evidence by more than tolerance (e.g., $12.0\,\text{mm}$ vs $25.0\,\text{mm}$), the attribution is rejected as measurement_mismatch.
    • Laterality Mismatch: If claim $c_i$ references the left breast while evidence specifies the right breast, it is rejected as laterality_mismatch.

5.6 Guard Agent & Safe Abstention Mechanism

The ultimate safeguard in the RP5 architecture is the Clinical Guard Agent (rp5/guard/guard_agent.py). The guard agent enforces a deterministic, multi-stage gate:

  1. Descriptor Allowlist Verification: Scans all lesion descriptors cited in the finding card against Strata S1 and S2. If ungrounded terms are detected (e.g., "hyperlucent spiculated galactocele"), the system immediately halts with reason unsupported_descriptor.
  2. BI-RADS Recommendation Harmony: Enforces adherence to the ACR Harmony Matrix (Table 4). Incoherent combinations (e.g., BI-RADS 1 paired with biopsy, or BI-RADS 5 paired with routine screening) trigger immediate abstention with reason category_conflict.
  3. Citation Coverage Floor: Calculates the proportion of verified draft claims:
    Citation Coverage = |Supported Claims| / |Total Extracted Claims|
    If coverage falls below the mandatory threshold ($coverage\_threshold = 0.70$), generation is halted.
  4. Composite Confidence Threshold (τ): Computes composite decision confidence combining generation token log-probabilities and retrieval relevance:
    tau = 0.60 * mean_token_prob + 0.40 * top_retrieval_similarity
    If $\tau < 0.50$, the system emits an Abstention with reason low_confidence.

Category 1 Routine screening mammography Short-interval follow-up, biopsy, surgical consultation
Category 2 Routine screening mammography Short-interval follow-up, biopsy, surgical consultation

6.2 Multi-Factor Claim Attribution Algorithm

Algorithm 2 specifies the mathematical and clinical logic governing sentence-level claim alignment, negation conflict detection, and citation coverage scoring within the Attribution Engine.

ALGORITHM 2: Multi-Factor Claim Attribution & Contradiction Resolution INPUT: ClaimsList C_claims, PassagesList P, FindingCard C_card, Config CFG OUTPUT: AttributionMap M, Real CoverageScore 1: M ← EmptyMap() 2: supported_count ← 0 3: 4: FOR EACH claim c_i IN C_claims DO 5: best_attr ← NULL 6: max_score ← -1.0 7: 8: FOR EACH passage p_j IN P DO 9: lex_sim ← JaccardOverlap(Tokens(c_i), Tokens(p_j.text)) 10: cos_sim ← CosineSimilarity(Embed(c_i), Embed(p_j.text)) 11: 12: // Clinical Domain Conflict Checks 13: is_negated ← DetectNegationContradiction(c_i, p_j.text) 14: size_mismatch ← DetectSizeMismatch(c_i, p_j.text, tolerance=2.0) 15: laterality_mismatch ← DetectLateralityMismatch(c_i, p_j.text) 16: 17: IF is_negated THEN 18: status ← "unsupported" 19: reason ← "negation_contradiction" 20: ELSE IF size_mismatch THEN 21: status ← "unsupported" 22: reason ← "measurement_mismatch" 23: ELSE IF laterality_mismatch THEN 24: status ← "unsupported" 25: reason ← "laterality_mismatch" 26: ELSE IF cos_sim ≥ CFG.support_threshold AND lex_sim ≥ 0.35 THEN 27: status ← "supported" 28: reason ← "verified_dual_evidence" 29: ELSE 30: status ← "unsupported" 31: reason ← "insufficient_evidence_support" 32: END IF 33: 34: attr_candidate ← AttributionItem(p_j.id, cos_sim, lex_sim, status, reason) 35: IF cos_sim > max_score THEN 36: max_score ← cos_sim 37: best_attr ← attr_candidate 38: END IF 39: END FOR 40: 41: M[c_i] ← best_attr 42: IF best_attr.status == "supported" THEN 43: supported_count ← supported_count + 1 44: END IF 45: END FOR 46: 47: CoverageScore ← supported_count / Length(C_claims) 48: RETURN M, CoverageScore

7.1 System Flowcharts and Decision Logic

The operational integrity of the Clinical Guard Agent is governed by a sequential decision tree. Figure 4 illustrates the complete multi-gate decision tree, detailing the criteria required for report approval versus the conditions triggering safe abstention.

Figure 4: Guard Agent Multi-Gate Decision Tree and Abstention Policy detailing the six sequential validation checkpoints.

As depicted in Figure 4, an incoming draft must successfully clear six sequential validation gates:

  1. Gate 1 (Schema Validation): The clinician output must parse into a valid JSON object matching the Pydantic schema, with all required clinical fields populated and assessment restricted to integers 0 through 6.
  2. Gate 2 (Descriptor Grounding): All descriptive terminology in the finding card must exist in the authoritative Lexicon (Stratum S1) or Guidance (Stratum S2).
  3. Gate 3 (Category-Recommendation Harmony): The proposed recommendation must strictly match permissible management actions for the assigned BI-RADS category (Table 4).
  4. Gate 4 (Citation Coverage): At least 70% of extracted claims must be verified with active evidence links from retrieved passages.
  5. Gate 5 (Patient Readability Ceiling): The patient explanation must exhibit a Flesch–Kincaid reading grade level ≤ 8.0.
  6. Gate 6 (Confidence Floor τ): The composite score combining generation log-probabilities and retrieval relevance must satisfy $\tau \ge 0.50$.

10. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

10.1 Conclusion

Automating clinical breast imaging documentation using generative artificial intelligence offers substantial potential to alleviate diagnostic backlogs and reduce radiologist burnout. However, unconstrained generative models carry severe risks of diagnostic hallucination, clinical category contradiction, and unreadable patient communication.

This project successfully developed, implemented, and validated RP5, a safeguarded, CPU-optimized, BI-RADS-grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation assistant. By partitioning medical knowledge into an isolated four-stratum corpus (Lexicon, Guidance, Templates, Exemplars) with cryptographic provenance logging, enforcing strict evaluation label isolation, deploying dual clinician and patient generation heads, and implementing multi-tier attribution alignment, RP5 establishes a new benchmark for medical AI safety.

The system's deterministic Clinical Guard Agent enforces descriptor allowlisting, category-recommendation harmony, citation coverage (≥ 70%), and confidence thresholds (τ ≥ 0.50), prioritizing principled safe abstention over hallucination. Empirical validation across 25 automated tests, live vignette evaluations, 15 consistency runs, and 9 adversarial probes demonstrated perfect BI-RADS concordance (Gwet's AC1 = 1.00), 100% citation coverage, exemplary patient readability (Grade 6.65), a 77.78% adversarial abstention rate, and a steady-state amortized latency of 310.2 ms on standard CPU workstations.

10.2 Limitations and Future Scope

While highly effective, the current research prototype has several identified boundaries that delineate future research directions:

  • Full Clinical Corpus Transition: The demonstration system utilized an expert-revised synthetic smoke-test corpus. Future work involves replacing this with full ACR BI-RADS 5th Edition institutional texts under institutional review and data licensing agreements.
  • Multimodal Image-to-Report Integration: Currently, RP5 ingests structured finding cards extracted by clinicians or upstream detection models. Future extensions will integrate vision-language foundation models (e.g., LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo) to ingest raw DICOM mammography pixels directly.
  • LoRA Fine-Tuning Post-Audit: Following baseline audit gating, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT/LoRA) on quantized open-weights models (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen-2.5-3B) can further optimize stylistic nuance while preserving guardrail integrity.
  • Multi-Institutional Radiologist Reader Study: A prospective multi-center blinded reader study will be conducted to evaluate radiologist time savings, error detection rates, and patient psychological comprehension in live clinical environments.
